## Operational maturity

The batch-platform capstone proves you can *build*; this one proves you can *run*.
That distinction — building vs. operating — is what separates a junior data engineer
from a senior one. Anyone can make a pipeline work on a good day; keeping it
trustworthy on a bad one is the harder, more valuable skill.

This capstone chains the full **incident lifecycle** into one flow:

- **Detect** — monitoring catches a breach before a stakeholder does (freshness/SLO).
- **Diagnose** — find the root cause, not its downstream victims.
- **Quantify** — measure business impact ("revenue was overstated by \$X"), because
  the first question leadership asks isn't "what broke" but "how bad."
- **Recover & verify** — fix, backfill, and *reconcile* before declaring done.

Running it as one exercise mirrors real **on-call**: an incident isn't four separate
skills, it's a single fluent motion under pressure. The maturity signals it drills —
symptom-based detection, root-cause discipline, impact-first communication,
verified recovery — are exactly what interviewers probe with "tell me about a time a
pipeline broke."

Reliability isn't the absence of failures (impossible); it's having a *practiced
response* to them. Turning that response into muscle memory is what operational
maturity means — and it's why this capstone exists alongside the build one.
